What the badge does

  • Renders inline where you paste it — no popups, no overlays.
  • Single outbound link to /r/<id> on orphograph.com. No extra fetches at page-load.
  • Self-contained: no extra CSS, no cookies, no analytics. Safe to embed on any privacy-conscious site.
  • Validates the receipt ID against [A-Za-z0-9_-]{1,64} before rendering — invalid IDs produce nothing, not a broken link.

Where to find your receipt ID

Every Orphograph receipt has a 12-character URL-safe ID like XwTULwlh76PcCst9. You can find it:

  • On the receipt page (/r/<id>) — it's the last path segment.
  • In the receipt JSON you downloaded — the receipt_id field.
  • In the email Resend sent you after anchoring (paid tiers).
  • On your account dashboard (Personal tier).

Multiple badges on one page

Each <script> tag renders a badge in its own position. You can place as many as you want — one per image works well in portfolio grids.

Tampering — what happens if someone steals my badge?

The badge is just a link. Anyone can copy it. What they cannot do is forge the receipt itself — the receipt proves a specific SHA-256 hash existed at a specific time, and that hash is whatever you originally anchored. If an attacker pastes your badge next to a different image, a visitor who clicks through and runs the verifier against the image gets a hash mismatch and can see the trick.

For higher-trust contexts (legal disputes, journalism), pair the badge with the open-source verifier link so visitors can confirm the file's hash matches independently. See github.com/orphograph/orphograph-verify.
