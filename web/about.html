About Orphograph

A solo-founder service for photographers, illustrators, and writers who want a permanent, cheap record that a file existed before a given date — before an AI training run, before a dispute, before a content claim.

Why this exists

Generative AI training is changing what "I made this" can mean. If your portfolio is online, it has probably been scraped. If a dispute ever comes up — "did this human make it, or is it an AI imitation?" — the practical answer is "whoever has the earliest verifiable timestamp."

Court-grade chain-of-custody is expensive and overkill for most working artists. A free Bitcoin-anchored cryptographic timestamp covers ~95% of real-world cases, and the protocol (OpenTimestamps) has existed since 2016. The gap was UX: dropping a file into a browser and walking away with a receipt your grandkids can still verify.

That's the entire product.

Who runs it

Orphograph is bootstrapped by a single operator. Solo means accountable — every choice on the site (the open-source verifier, the receipts that outlive the company, the no-fingerprinting analytics) is one person's call, made deliberately, not a committee compromise.

Solo also means honest about scale. We are not a 24/7 enterprise support desk. Email replies arrive within a few days, not within minutes. The software is durable; the human running it has a normal schedule.

The trust contract

Every commercial timestamping service before us has eventually died, pivoted, or been acquired. Proof of Existence, Stampery, Po.et, OriginStamp's consumer tier — all of them issued receipts that became worthless when the company moved on.

We refuse to make that trade. Concretely:

  1. The receipt format is open. Standard OpenTimestamps .ots files plus a small JSON manifest. No proprietary fields, no DRM, no "premium verification" tier.
  2. The verifier is MIT-licensed. ~100 lines of Python, stdlib only, hosted at orphograph.com/verify/. Download a copy, throw it on your backup drive. If we vanish, your receipt still verifies.
  3. Your files never touch our server. SHA-256 is computed in your browser via the WebCrypto API. We only ever see the 32-byte hash, never the file bytes. Verifiable: open the network tab, drop a file, watch what gets sent.
  4. Bitcoin is the durable layer, not us. The receipts anchor to public Bitcoin transactions via the OpenTimestamps calendar network. Five independent calendars per anchor. Even if all five calendars disappear, an "upgraded" .ots file contains the full Merkle path to a Bitcoin block — no calendars needed for verification.

What we don't do

  • We don't claim "legally binding" or "court-admissible." A timestamp tells you a hash existed at a moment in time. Whether that's evidence depends on jurisdiction, judge, and procedure — way above our pay grade. We sell proof of existence, not legal evidence.
  • We don't host your files. If you lose the file, the receipt by itself is useless — there's nothing to compare against. Back up your files like you always have.
  • We don't watermark, fingerprint, or track. No Google Analytics, no Mixpanel, no third-party scripts at all. The only request log is the server's own access log, which captures truncated IPs and is rotated weekly.
  • We don't do qualified TSA / eIDAS. If you need an EU-qualified electronic timestamp for regulated contracts, services like Bernstein.io serve that market.

What happens if Orphograph shuts down

Read this post for the deep version. The short version: your receipts still work, because the trust is in Bitcoin and an open standard, not in us. Save the verifier locally and you are independent of our continued existence.
