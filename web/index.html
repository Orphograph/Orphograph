In plain language, what is this?

Imagine you took a photo this morning. You want a way to prove to anyone, forever, that this exact photo existed before this exact moment. Not "I uploaded it to Instagram" (Instagram can edit the timestamp, or vanish, or delete your account). Something that doesn't depend on any single company — including ours — continuing to exist.

That's what this site does. Drop a file. We compute a small cryptographic fingerprint of it — right inside your browser, so the file itself never leaves your machine — and we anchor that fingerprint to the Bitcoin blockchain. The Bitcoin chain is run by ~50,000 independent computers worldwide; once your fingerprint is in it, the only way for someone to remove the proof would be to convince every one of them. That's never happened in 16 years.

You get a small receipt file (~3 KB). Save it next to your photo. Five years from now, anyone with your photo + your receipt can verify it against the Bitcoin chain in 10 seconds — even if our website is long gone. The receipt is yours, the proof is yours, and you don't need to trust us to read it.

Why you'd want this

You make art and AI is being trained on it

If your work shows up in a generative AI dataset, you may need to prove it was made before the model was trained. A Bitcoin timestamp from before the training cutoff is hard for anyone to argue with.

You're a journalist or photographer with sensitive material

Documents leaked to you, photos from a protest, a recording that might be challenged later — anchor the originals immediately. If anyone claims the file was created or altered after the fact, your receipt says otherwise.

You delivered creative work to a client

Anchor the final deliverable on delivery day. If a dispute comes up later — "this isn't what we agreed to" — you have cryptographic proof of what you actually sent and when.

You're documenting something for the long term

Family archive, oral history, research data, anything where the date matters and you want it to still be verifiable in 30 years without depending on any company surviving that long.

How to use it — your first anchor in 3 steps

  1. Drop a file into the box below. Any file works — photo, PDF, MP3, ZIP, code, whatever. The file itself never leaves your laptop; we only ever see a 64-character fingerprint.
  2. Wait about 10 seconds. We send the fingerprint to five independent Bitcoin timestamp servers. You'll see green checkmarks appear as each one confirms.
  3. Download your receipt. It's a tiny file (~3 KB). Save it next to the original file you anchored. That's it — your proof exists, forever.

To check a receipt later, scroll to Verify below or use the open-source command-line tool. Both work even if our site disappears.

How it works

  
    
    
  1. Your browser computes the SHA-256 of your file using crypto.subtle — the file never leaves your machine.
    2. 
    
  2. The 32-byte hash is sent to our server, which submits it to five OpenTimestamps calendars.
    3. 
    
  3. Each calendar aggregates many hashes into a single Merkle root and writes it to a Bitcoin transaction (~hourly).
    4. 
    
  4. You receive a receipt with the hash, timestamp, and five independently-verifiable OTS proofs.

  5. To prove existence later, anyone can re-hash the original file and check the receipt — no trust in us required.

FAQ

  

    
How is this different from OpenTimestamps?

    

      OpenTimestamps is the protocol we use under the hood — it's free if you're
      comfortable with a Python CLI. Orphograph gives you a browser flow, an emailed
      receipt, and a verifier that anyone can run without installing Python. The
      cryptography is the same; the wedge is convenience and receipt UX.
    


    
What if your domain disappears?

    

      Your receipt JSON + 5 .ots proof files + our open-source verifier
      (link) keep working without us. Save them
      locally — that's all you need. The proofs are anchored to Bitcoin's chain,
      not to our server.
    


    
Is this legal evidence?

    

      No. We provide proof-of-existence — strong cryptographic evidence that a file
      existed at a moment in time. We are not a qualified
      trust-service provider (no eIDAS, no court-admissibility guarantee). For
      litigation, consult a digital evidence specialist. Treat us as a sturdy
      "this existed by date X" witness, not as a notary replacement.
    


    
Can I embed a "verified" badge on my portfolio?

    

      Yes — one line of HTML pastes a small "Anchored to Bitcoin · view
      receipt" badge that links to the receipt page. No tracking, no
      external fetches. See the badge
      demo for the snippet.
    


    
Who's behind this?

    

      Built by an anonymous solo founder. The code is small (under 1,000
      lines of Python + vanilla JS, stdlib only). The verifier is open source so
      anyone can audit it and so your proofs outlive the company.
    


    
Is this quantum-safe?

    

      The proof binding (your file hash → Merkle root → Bitcoin block)
      is purely hash-based, so it is not exposed to Shor's algorithm. SHA-256
      under Grover's algorithm retains roughly 128-bit effective security —
      enough that NIST still considers it acceptable.
    

    

      As a hedge, every receipt now also stores a SHA-512 sibling witness
      computed in your browser. To forge a file matching your receipt, an
      attacker would need a simultaneous collision in both SHA-256 and
      SHA-512 — substantially harder than either alone, even with a
      capable quantum computer. The OTS Bitcoin anchor still uses SHA-256
      (calendar protocol requirement); the SHA-512 sibling is independent
      and rides along in the receipt.
    


    
What about EXIF / capture metadata?

    

      Today we anchor the hash of whatever bytes you drop in. We don't read EXIF
      and we don't bind your identity to the file — you bring your own metadata.
      A capture-time provenance tier is on the roadmap; we'd rather ship it
      working than promise it now.
