The Procedure

  1. Place a file at the counter. Your browser reads it locally.
  2. Your browser computes its SHA-256 and SHA-512 fingerprints. The file does not move.
  3. The fingerprints are submitted to five independent OpenTimestamps calendars.
  4. You receive a 16-character receipt identifier and five small .ots proof files.
  5. Within roughly one hour the fingerprint is committed to a Bitcoin block. The receipt's status flips from pending to confirmed.

The Office

Orphograph is an empirical notary. Its sole function is to record, in a manner mathematically permanent and independently verifiable, that a file existed on or before a specific block of the Bitcoin blockchain. The office issues no opinion on authorship, ownership, or legality. It issues only the fact.

What Crosses the Counter

Only the SHA-256 fingerprint of your file crosses the counter — a 64-character number that is one-way and content-blind. The file itself remains on your machine and is read by your browser alone. The office cannot reconstruct it, identify it, or transmit it. There is no copy to retrieve and none was ever collected.

Why the Chain

Bitcoin has not reorganized beyond a handful of blocks in sixteen years of operation. A block from one hour ago is, for every practical purpose, immutable. By submitting your fingerprint to five independent OpenTimestamps calendars, we link it through public mathematics to a block whose proof-of-work cannot be undone.

What Survives the Office

The receipts. If this office closes, changes hands, or is forgotten, every receipt it ever issued continues to verify against the chain using open-source software. There is no proprietary verifier, no license key, no captive format. The instrument outlives the institution. That is the contract.

What the Office Refuses

Claims of court admissibility. Promises of legal weight. Statements about authorship. Sale of customer identity. Per-receipt blockchain fees. Hidden lock-in. Marketing language untethered from the cryptography. Anything that would require the customer to trust us in order for the instrument to work.

Why It Is Cheap

OpenTimestamps batches many fingerprints into a single Bitcoin transaction roughly once per hour. The marginal on-chain cost of one additional fingerprint is, in arithmetic terms, near zero. This is why the first three recordings every twenty-four hours are free — not as inducement, but as architecture.

Method I · One Click

Open the receipt page

Every receipt has a public URL: /r/<id>. The page shows the fingerprint, calendar status, the Bitcoin block once mined, and a drop-zone to re-hash any file and check the match in your browser.

Method II · Open Source

Run the open-source verifier

A small standalone tool, on your computer. Hashes the file, reads the .ots proof files, talks to the OpenTimestamps calendars, reports pass or fail. How →

Method III · Trustless

On legality. Orphograph is not a notary public, nor a registered timestamping authority under eIDAS or equivalent statute. Its receipts are verifiable cryptographic facts, not legal opinions. Admissibility in any jurisdiction is determined by counsel and the relevant court — not by this office.

On privacy. Files are hashed in the customer's browser and never transmitted. The office retains no copy of the original. The receipt contains the fingerprint, the time of submission, and the calendar attestations — nothing more, unless the customer voluntarily attaches metadata.

On survival. Receipts verify by reference to the Bitcoin blockchain, using open-source software (OpenTimestamps) that does not depend on this office continuing to exist.

On the guarantee. The privacy contract of this service is structural, not promissory. Your file is never transmitted, by anyone, by any tool, to any party. The architecture cannot disclose what it never received — and that property holds regardless of who reads, writes, or maintains the code.
