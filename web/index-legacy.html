What is this, in plain English

Imagine you took a photo this morning and need to prove, years from now, that it existed today. A social-media post date is not durable evidence: accounts can lapse, platforms change, timestamps can be edited. You want something that doesn't depend on any one company, including ours.

You drop a file. Your browser computes a unique fingerprint of it — the file itself never leaves your machine. We register that fingerprint to Bitcoin, the most-attacked, most-survived database in history. You download a tiny receipt. That's it.

Bitcoin is maintained by ~50,000 independent computers worldwide. To erase your proof, someone would need to convince every one of them. That's never happened in 16 years. Your receipt verifies against the chain directly — you don't need us alive to read it.

How it works

  1. Drop a file. Your browser computes a fingerprint locally — the file stays on your machine.
  2. Wait ~10 seconds. Five independent timestamp servers confirm. Green checkmarks appear as each one finishes.
  3. Download your receipt. A 3 KB file. Save it next to your original. Done.
Show the technical detail
  1. Your browser computes the SHA-256 of your file using crypto.subtle.digest — the file never leaves your machine.
  2. The 32-byte hash is sent to our server, which submits it to five OpenTimestamps calendars (a.pool, b.pool, alice, finney, btc.catallaxy).
  3. Each calendar aggregates many hashes into a single Merkle root and writes it to a Bitcoin transaction (~hourly).
  4. You receive a receipt with the hash, timestamp, and five independently-verifiable OTS proofs.
  5. To prove existence later, anyone can re-hash the original file and check the receipt — no trust in us required.
  6. We also embed an optional SHA-512 sibling witness in receipts as a quantum-era hedge.

FAQ

  

    
Is this legal evidence?

    

      Strong evidence that a file existed at a specific moment. Useful for
      deterring disputes, establishing priority of creation, and supporting
      other evidence in negotiation or court. Not a replacement for a notary —
      we're not a qualified trust service provider under eIDAS, and we don't
      guarantee admissibility. For litigation, talk to a digital evidence
      specialist.
    


    
What happens if your site disappears?

    

      Your receipt and the five OTS proof files keep working. The
      open-source verifier
      reads them without us. The proofs live on Bitcoin's chain, not our
      server. Save the receipt locally; that's all you need.
    


    
How is this different from a social-media post date or sending myself an email?

    

      Platform timestamps depend on the platform continuing to exist and on
      its internal records. Email timestamps depend on email providers.
      A Bitcoin timestamp depends on no single party — it is verified by
      tens of thousands of independent computers and has not been rewritten
      in sixteen years.
    


    
Do you ever see my file?

    

      No. SHA-256 is computed in your browser. We only ever receive a
      64-character fingerprint. There is no upload of the file itself.
    


    
How is this different from OpenTimestamps directly?

    

      OpenTimestamps is the protocol we use. It's free if you're comfortable
      with a Python CLI. We provide a browser flow, emailed receipts, a hosted
      verifier, and a badge system. The cryptography is identical; you're
      paying for convenience.
    


    
Can I embed a verified badge?

    

      Yes — one line of HTML. See the
      badge section above.
    


    
Is this quantum-safe?

    

      The proof binding is hash-based, which resists quantum attacks far
      better than signature schemes. We also embed an optional SHA-512 sibling witness
      in receipts as an extra hedge.
      Technical detail at /security.
    


    
Who's behind this?

    

      Built and operated by a small team committed to keeping the codebase
      auditable and the surface area small. The verifier is open source so
      your proofs outlive any operator — including us. For sensitive use
      cases (legal, journalism), contact us at
      hello@orphograph.com for
      additional context.
