Timestamp source documents before publishing

For investigative journalists, fact-checkers, and editors who need a defensible record of when a file came into their possession.

Journalism that breaks news on leaked documents lives or dies on provenance. The moment a story runs, a hostile party will claim the documents are fakes, were altered, or were created after the fact to fit a narrative. Most of those claims can be defeated with a single cryptographic anchor: a Bitcoin timestamp on the source file, dated to before the story published.

The minimum viable workflow

As soon as a document, recording, or photo arrives from a source, before any editing or transcription:

  1. Make a working copy. Set the original aside on read-only media.
  2. Drop the working copy into Orphograph. SHA-256 runs in your browser; the bytes never upload. You receive a Bitcoin-anchored receipt within minutes.
  3. Store the receipt with your source file in your secure archive. Note in your reporter's log when the file arrived, from whom (per your own source-protection scheme), and the anchor receipt ID.

If the document's authenticity is ever challenged, you can point to the receipt and demonstrate: some file with these exact bytes was in my possession before the story ran. That defeats the "you made it up after publication" attack. It does not defeat the "your source faked the document before giving it to you" attack — no timestamp can — but it sharply narrows the dispute space.

Privacy is structural

Orphograph hashes locally via WebCrypto. We never see the document's contents. We never see file names unless you check the "include filename in receipt" box. We log the truncated IP for abuse prevention and the hash itself. For high-stakes operations, anchor over Tor or via a VPN; the hash itself is unlinkable to contents anyway, but operational anonymity always helps.

Anchor early, anchor often

The value of a timestamp is its earliness. A receipt from the morning the document arrived is more useful than a receipt from the night before publication. Build the anchor into your intake ritual the same way you'd build encrypted-comms into your contact ritual. Free tier covers one file per month; the $7 Pack covers ten receipts for newsrooms doing occasional bulk intake.

What this is not

Not legal evidence on its own. Not source protection — that is a function of your tradecraft, not of any timestamping tool. Not document authentication — the timestamp proves the file existed, not that the file's claims are true. For high-risk reporting, pair the timestamp with a media-law consult and your newsroom's standard evidence-handling procedures.

FAQ

Do the source documents upload anywhere?

No. SHA-256 runs in your browser; only the 32-byte hash reaches the anchor service.

Will this protect my source?

It protects the document. Source protection is a function of your operational security — encrypted comms, air-gapped storage, no metadata leaks.

Can I anchor a folder of documents at once?

Zip or tar the folder and anchor the archive. The receipt covers everything inside, and the archive's manifest preserves each file's identity.

What if I'm subpoenaed for the receipt?

The receipt is just a hash, a timestamp, and Merkle paths. It does not reveal the document's contents.

Is the receipt by itself enough for legal protection?

No. Pair it with a media-law consult and your newsroom's chain-of-custody procedures.
