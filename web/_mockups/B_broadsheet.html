The Office

Orphograph is an empirical notary. Its sole function is to record, in a manner mathematically permanent and independently verifiable, that a file existed on or before a specific block of the Bitcoin blockchain. The office issues no opinion on authorship, ownership, or legality. It issues only the fact.

What Crosses the Counter

Only the SHA-256 fingerprint of your file crosses the counter — a 64-character number that is one-way and content-blind. The file itself remains on your machine and is read by your browser alone. The office cannot reconstruct it, identify it, or transmit it. There is no copy to lose, no archive to breach.

Why the Chain

Bitcoin has not reorganized beyond a handful of blocks in sixteen years of operation. A block from one hour ago is, for every practical purpose, immutable. By submitting your fingerprint to five independent OpenTimestamps calendars, we link it through public mathematics to a block whose proof-of-work cannot be undone.

What Survives the Office

The receipts. If this office closes, dies, or is forgotten, every receipt it ever issued continues to verify against the chain using open-source software. There is no proprietary verifier, no license, no key the office retains. The instrument outlives the institution. That is the contract.

What the Office Refuses

Claims of court admissibility. Promises of legal weight. Statements about authorship. Sale of customer identity. Per-receipt blockchain fees. Hidden lock-in. Marketing language untethered from the cryptography. Anything that would require the customer to trust us in order for the instrument to work.

Why It Is Cheap

OpenTimestamps batches many fingerprints into a single Bitcoin transaction once per hour. The marginal on-chain cost of one additional fingerprint is, in arithmetic terms, zero. This is why the first three recordings every twenty-four hours are free — not as inducement, but as architecture.

The Procedure

  1. Place a file in the counter at right. Your browser reads it locally.
  2. Your browser computes its SHA-256 fingerprint. The file does not move.
  3. The fingerprint is submitted to five OpenTimestamps calendars.
  4. You receive a receipt (a sixteen-character identifier) and five small .ots proof files.
  5. Within roughly one hour, the fingerprint is committed to a Bitcoin block. The receipt's status flips from pending to confirmed.

On legality. Orphograph does not assert that its receipts are court-admissible, legally binding, or substitutes for a sworn notary public. Its instrument is a verifiable cryptographic fact. The legal use of that fact in any jurisdiction is determined by counsel and the relevant court, not by this office.

On privacy. Files are hashed in the customer's browser and never transmitted. The office retains no copy of the original. The receipt contains the fingerprint, the time of submission, and the calendar attestations — nothing more, unless the customer voluntarily attaches metadata.

On survival. Receipts verify by reference to the Bitcoin blockchain, using open-source software (OpenTimestamps) that does not depend on this office continuing to exist. The instrument is portable to any future verifier.
