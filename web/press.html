Story angles

Five framings, each pointed at a different desk. Mix and match.

  • Consumer

    The AI-trust crisis in photography

    Between roughly 30% and 70% of new online images now show signs of generative AI. Working photographers are being asked, casually and constantly, "is this real?" Orphograph is the cheap, durable answer that doesn't depend on a platform staying alive.

  • Tech

    Capture-time provenance: the next stage past C2PA

    C2PA signs metadata that travels with a file and can be re-signed. A Bitcoin anchor commits a hash to a chain no single company controls. The Creator tier extends that anchor to the shutter press itself, not after-the-fact upload.

  • Business

    Bootstrapped vs venture in 2026

    Built solo and anonymously, on Python stdlib and vanilla JS, with no outside funding. Realistic target: $500–$2,000 MRR in twelve months. A small, profitable, durable trust product in a category where most peers raised, pivoted, and disappeared.

  • Meta

    Founder transparency: an AI-trust product written largely by AI

    Most of the codebase and most of the launch essay were written by an LLM, reviewed by the founder, and disclosed at the top of every page. The disclosure paradox — disclosing a credibility-undermining fact increases trust — is the design principle.

  • Security

    Privacy by construction: a product that physically can't leak

    The file's bytes never reach the server. SHA-256 is computed in the browser via WebCrypto; only the 32-byte fingerprint is transmitted. There is no plaintext file to subpoena, breach, or hand over — because none was ever collected.

FAQ for journalists

How is this different from C2PA?

C2PA attaches signed metadata to a file; the metadata can be re-signed by anyone with credentials and travels only as long as the file format preserves it. Orphograph commits a SHA-256 fingerprint to the Bitcoin blockchain — the proof exists independent of the file format, the metadata, and the company. The two are complementary, not competitive.

Have you raised money?

No. Solo-founder bootstrapped. No outside investors, no advisors with equity, no incubator. Hosting and domain costs are under $30 a month.

How big is the team?

One person, with substantial AI-collaborative development openly disclosed across the site and the launch essay.

What's the founder's background?

The founder publishes as Orphograph and prefers to keep personal details off the record. Orphograph is bootstrapped on personal time. Inquiries: hello@orphograph.com.

Is this legal evidence?

No, and we say so on every page that touches the question. Orphograph provides cryptographic proof-of-existence — strong evidence that a file existed at a specific moment. It is not a qualified trust-service provider and does not market itself as a notary or evidentiary replacement. Litigation should involve a digital-evidence specialist.

What's the moat?

Three layers. First, UX: most OpenTimestamps usage today requires a Python CLI; Orphograph turns it into a browser drop zone with an emailed receipt. Second, the disclosure-and-trust posture: open verifier, file never uploads, AI-collaboration disclosed, receipts that work without us. Third, the Creator tier — capture-time provenance — moves the anchor moment from upload to shutter press.

Why Bitcoin and not a database or a permissioned chain?

The whole point is that the proof outlives the company. A database we control is the weakest possible witness. Bitcoin is the most-attacked, longest-running public blockchain on earth; once a hash is committed to a block, undoing it would require convincing tens of thousands of independent operators worldwide. That's the strength the receipt inherits.

What's next?

Three things in the pipeline: the Creator tier (capture-time companion app), a small developer-facing API and verifier badge for portfolio sites, and a Claude Code plugin for anchoring files from inside an editor session. None of those change the non-negotiable guarantees: client-side hashing, batched anchoring, open-source verifier, receipts that work without us.
